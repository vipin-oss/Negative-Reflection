**Note on physical interpretation.** Memory-dependent derivatives (a time-delayed kernel on a

single interface, and the "negative reflection" test must be stated on the flux, not on the sign of
a real amplitude ratio.

## 3. Critical assessment: what is established, what is not

### 3.1 Established (evidence: the publications above)

- **Flux-partition bookkeeping at coupled interfaces.** Repeated, with explicit energy-ratio
  checks (#1–#5, #9, #10). This is the single most transferable capability for the proposed topic.
- **Coupled-field constitutive modelling** — piezoelectric, piezomagnetic, hygro-thermal,
  viscoelastic, porous — with consistent derivation of governing equations (#13–#42).
- **Nonlocal / strain-gradient / memory / fractional dispersion relations**, including
  numerical root extraction (Durand–Kerner appears in #15; characteristic-equation root
  classification in several others).
- **Interface and imperfect-contact modelling** in cylindrical and layered geometries (#32, #35).
- **Graded (functionally graded) property variation** in piezomagnetic and piezoelectric media
  (#33, #34, #35).

### 3.2 NOT established in his corpus (verified absence, not an assumption)

I searched his 42-work record for combinations of {negative reflection, backward wave, zero group
velocity, mode conversion, forward–backward, Lamb wave, guided plate, edge scattering}:

- **No paper on forward/backward guided-mode conversion.**
- **No paper on negative reflection, negative refraction, or back-focusing.**
- **No paper computing a modal scattering matrix at a plate edge or a waveguide junction.**
- **No paper on zero-group-velocity points.**
- **No paper on Lamb waves in a plate with a free edge** (his plate work is guided waves in
  cylindrical shells or porous seabeds, not the finite-edge scattering problem).
- **No experimental work of any kind.** The entire corpus is analytical/semi-analytical.
- **No strain-gradient boundary-layer analysis**: his strain-gradient appearances are in
  dispersion relations, not in a higher-order boundary condition set.

Two consequences follow, and they pull in opposite directions:

1. **Feasibility is high for the mathematics.** The constitutive machinery, root-finding, and
   energy bookkeeping are entirely familiar to him. A negative-reflection problem in a coupled
   medium would be a natural extension of his own toolkit.
2. **Feasibility is low for the numerics that decide the question.** The decisive quantity —
   the edge reflection matrix, including a large set of evanescent and inhomogeneous modes,
   with Moore–Penrose inversion of an ill-conditioned modal basis — is not present anywhere in
   his record. This is the main skill gap and the main risk to any proposal, and it is addressed
   explicitly in `08_Mathematical_Feasibility_Audit.md`.

### 3.3 Publication venue profile (factual, not evaluative)

His venues cluster in *Mechanics of Advanced Materials and Structures*, *International Journal of
Numerical Methods for Heat & Fluid Flow*, *Physica Scripta*, *European Journal of Mechanics A/Solids*,
*Composite Structures*, *Journal of Thermal Stresses*, *Waves in Random and Complex Media*.
His work has **not** appeared in the physics-of-waves venues where the negative-reflection
literature lives (*Physical Review B/Applied*, *JASA*, *Wave Motion*, *JSV*, *Scientific Reports*
being the exception where he has published twice).

This is a real strategic constraint on problem design: a proposal that is *only* a coupled-material
extension of an established elastic result will read as incremental to a *Wave Motion* or *PRB*
referee, whereas one that establishes a new *mechanism* has a chance. This consideration drives
the ranking in `09_Comparative_Research_Ranking.md`.

## 6. Bottom line for the committee

Dr. Gupta has a **strong, coherent, verified** record in coupled-field wave propagation with a
genuine specialism in interface energy partitioning and in nonlocal/memory-dependent dispersion.
He has **no** record in guided-mode edge scattering, forward–backward conversion, or zero-group-velocity
physics, and no experimental programme.

A viable problem therefore has to satisfy three constraints simultaneously:

1. the *constitutive* content must be in his established strength (coupled media, grading,
   porosity, nonlocality);
2. the *scattering machinery* must be built essentially from scratch, so it must be kept as
   simple as the physics allows — this argues for a 2-D plate with a straight edge rather than a
   cylindrical shell or a 3-D junction;
3. the *claim* must be about a mechanism, not merely about a material substitution, or it will be
   judged incremental.
